MCLAREN

THE FASTEST PAPAYA IN THE WORLD

Explore the history
1963

The Opening

In 1963, Bruce McLaren founded Bruce McLaren Motor Racing after leaving Cooper.

1966–1970

Entering the Peak of Motorsports

In 1966, the team entered Formula 1, specifically Monaco. Bruce scored their first team win at Spa in 1968 driving the iconic papaya-orange McLaren M7A. Not only F1, they also dominated North-American Can-Am, winning 5 consecutive constructors' titles (1967–1971). Tragedy struck on June 2, 1970, when Bruce was killed testing a Can-Am car (McLaren M8D) at Goodwood. He was 32 years old.

1974–1976

The First Titles and Territorial Expansions

After Bruce's death, the team was led by American entrepreneur Teddy Mayer. The grieving team pushed forward, and with the clean, reliable M23 chassis they won their first titles, both constructors and drivers, in 1974 with Emerson Fittipaldi, followed by James Hunt's dramatic championship against Niki Lauda in 1976. Not only Can-Am, they also conquered the Indianapolis 500 in 1974 and 1976 with Johnny Rutherford.

1980s

The Project Four

Entering the 1980s, McLaren merged with Ron Dennis's Project Four, which is why every McLaren chassis since has been named "MP4". Designer John Barnard introduced the MP4/1 in 1981, the first F1 car with a full carbon monocoque, revolutionizing motorsport safety and stiffness. With TAG-Porsche and later Honda turbo engines, Niki Lauda (1984), Alain Prost (1985, 1986, 1989) and Ayrton Senna (1988, 1990, 1991) forged a golden dynasty. In 1988, the MP4/4 won 15 out of 16 races.

1992–1999

Triple Crown and Record Breaking!

In 1992, designer Gordon Murray translated his F1 obsession into "art on wheels": the legendary McLaren F1. It features a central driving seat and a gold-lined engine bay with a naturally-aspirated 6.1L BMW V12. It hit 386 km/h (240.1 mph), becoming the world's fastest production car. In 1995, the racing version, the F1 GTR, shocked the world by winning the 24 Hours of Le Mans on its debut, securing motorsport's unofficial Triple Crown (Monaco, Indy 500, Le Mans). Back in F1, Mika Häkkinen delivered back-to-back titles in 1998 and 1999.

2000s

Silver Arrow Team-Up and New Technology Centre

Entering the 2000s, McLaren teamed with the Silver Arrows, Mercedes-Benz, iconic for its silver-chrome livery. They stayed a front-runner through Kimi Räikkönen's near-misses and Lewis Hamilton's dramatic last-corner 2008 World Championship. Off track, McLaren opened the futuristic McLaren Technology Centre in Woking and launched McLaren Automotive in 2010.

2010s

More Production Cars!

Entering the 2010s, McLaren released two supercars: the MP4-12C and the P1. The 12C was revealed online in 2009, made its public debut at the Goodwood Festival of Speed in 2010, and went into production in 2011. In 2012, McLaren revealed the P1 concept at the Paris Motor Show; it entered production in 2013 and ended in 2015.

2015–2025

Start Over!

In 2015, McLaren reunited with Honda (2015–2017) and was thrown to the back of the constructors' championship. Under CEO Zak Brown and team principal Andrea Stella, McLaren started over from scratch. The payoff arrived in 2024, when Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri clinched McLaren's first Constructors' Championship in 26 years. The peak came in 2025 with the double championship, both driver and constructor, and the unveiling of the 1,257 hp McLaren W1 hypercar.
